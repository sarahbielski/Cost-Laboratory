See the formulas and interpretation

Contribution per unit = price − unit variable cost. Profit = revenue − variable costs − fixed costs. Single-product break-even = fixed costs ÷ contribution, rounded UP to whole units. Break-even revenue and margin of safety use the unrounded threshold.

Multiple products use your quantity-weighted average contribution and price. Break-even assumes the same mix; fractional product quantities are a planning approximation. Shared fixed costs are allocated once by units or revenue. With no sales mix, break-even is undefined. Product-level allocated profit is not an independent break-even forecast.

Startup cash includes inventory funding and capital purchases. Equipment depreciation = purchase cost ÷ useful life in months; cash-only startup costs are excluded from operating break-even. Annual operating amounts ÷ 12 normalize to months. Annualized variable cost uses the assumed monthly sales × 12, not a forecast.

Transparent rules assess this model—not the probability of business failure. You make the final decision.

How risk classifications work

Financially unsustainable: any product contribution ≤ 0. High: operating loss, margin of safety below 20%/undefined, a loss after +10% costs or −25% sales, a missing essential Production/Logistics/Payments category, or an illustrative cost ≥10% of operating costs. Moderate: otherwise, unverified costs remain. Lower: all those checks clear. All cases still carry uncertain demand and untested performance warnings.
